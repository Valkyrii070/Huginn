# Settings
Everything about this computer, on one page each. Search at the top right finds any setting.

## Find a setting
Type in Search settings: "night light", "password", "VPN". Pick a result to go straight to its page. Each page has its own colour, and the window takes it on.

## Wi‑Fi and network
Turn Wi‑Fi on or off, pick a network and type its password once (the system keeps it). Join another network covers ones that hide their name. This network shows your IP address, router and DNS. Add a VPN from the WireGuard (.conf) or OpenVPN (.ovpn) file your provider gives you. Personal hotspot shares a wired connection with your phone; Settings tells you the password it made.

## Bluetooth
Put the device in pairing mode (usually by holding its button), choose Look for devices, then Pair. My devices shows what's paired, whether it's connected and its battery.

## Displays
With two screens or more, drag them on the map to match your desk. For each screen: resolution, size of things (scaling), refresh rate and turning. Night light makes colours warmer in the evening, from sunset to sunrise, at set hours, or always.

## Sound
Choose where sound plays and which microphone listens, and how loud each is. Apps playing now lets you turn one app down without touching the rest.

## Battery and power
The ring shows the charge; under it, the battery's health (how much it holds compared with new) and its charge cycles. Power mode trades speed for battery. Choose when the screen turns off and the computer sleeps, plugged in and on battery, and what closing the lid and the power button do.

## Keyboard and mouse
Add keyboard layouts and switch between them with Meta+Space. Make Caps Lock another Control or Escape, turn on the Compose key, and set how keys repeat. Each touchpad and mouse has its own speed, natural scrolling, tap to click and left-handed buttons.

## Date, time and language
Pick your time zone and whether time servers keep the clock right. Language sets what the system speaks; Formats sets how dates, numbers and money look, with a sample underneath. Language changes take effect the next time you sign in.

## Users
Change your picture (Huginn draws some from your initials, or pick a photo), your name and your password. Administrators can add people: they choose their own password the first time they sign in. The system asks for your password before adding or removing anyone.

## Default apps
Each tile is a kind of file: click it to choose which app opens it. Open when you sign in lists apps that start by themselves.

## Privacy and security
The firewall keeps everything outside from reaching this computer unless you allow it. What apps may use lists the camera, microphone, location and more that apps from Flathub have asked for: switch any off, or Reset to be asked again. Screen lock, recent files and emptying the Trash by itself are here too.

## Notifications
Do Not Disturb holds every banner and sound; quiet hours turn it on by itself at night. Each app has three switches: allowed, banners, sounds.

## Appearance and wallpaper
Light or dark, and the realm. Each realm has its own living scene for a wallpaper: Aurora's northern lights,
Bifröst's bridge of light, Muspelheim's fire and Niflheim's ice. It moves on the sign-in screen and comes to rest
after you sign in. Turn on **Live wallpaper** to keep it moving on the desktop too (it uses a little more power, and
stays still when Reduce motion is on). **Sign-in screen** shows which realm everyone sees before signing in; **Use …**
changes it to yours, after the administrator password.

## Accessibility
Seeing: the screen reader (Orca) reads out what's on the screen; Zoom magnifies around the pointer (follow it, keep it centred, or push at the edges); Make everything bigger; High contrast; Solid surfaces instead of glass; Reduce motion; colour filters for colour blindness, or grey; and the pointer's size. Hearing: flash the screen instead of the alert sound. Moving and typing: sticky keys (shortcuts one key at a time), slow keys (a key must be held a moment), bounce keys (ignore quick repeats), moving the pointer with the number keys, and the on-screen keyboard.
